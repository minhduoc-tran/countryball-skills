# 81 ngày đêm Thành cổ Quảng Trị: Research Facts
## Siege of Quảng Trị Citadel 1972 (28 June – 16 September)

---

## 1. Context: Easter Offensive 1972 & Initial Capture

### Easter Offensive Launch (30 March 1972)
- **Trigger**: North Vietnamese strategy to capture South Vietnam territory before Paris peace talks; PAVN bypassed DMZ
- **First Battle of Quảng Trị (30 March – 2 May 1972)**:
  - NVA overran province; captured Quảng Trị city 2 May 1972
  - Made Quảng Trị **first province completely liberated** (theo phía Việt Nam: "tỉnh đầu tiên giải phóng hoàn toàn")
  - ARVN 3rd Division collapsed; rapid retreat down Route 1
  - "Đại lộ kinh hoàng" (Highway 1 of horrors): massive refugee exodus southward
  - Source: [Wikipedia – First Battle of Quảng Trị; HistoryNet – Easter Offensive of 1972](https://historynet.com/how-the-1972-north-vietnamese-easter-offensive-tested-nixons-war-strategy/)
- **Confidence**: HIGH

### Nixon's Response: Linebacker I (9 May 1972 onward)
- **Bombing campaign initiated** after NVA Easter Offensive
- **Harbor mining** of North Vietnamese ports announced 8 May
- **Objective**: Restrict NVA resupply, degrade war capacity
- **Paris talks stalled** — Oct 1972 draft agreement nearly finalized but fell apart Nov-Dec 1972
- Source: [Military.com – Operation Linebacker I](https://www.military.com/daily-news/investigations-and-features/2025/12/09/operation-linebacker-ii-bombing-campaign-forced-north-vietnam-peace-table-1973.html)
- **Confidence**: HIGH

## 2. Counter-Offensive: Operation Lam Sơn 72 (28 June – 16 September 1972)

### Command Structure & Commander
- **I Corps Commander**: Lt. Gen. Ngô Quang Trưởng (assumed command 2 May 1972, replacing Gen. Hoàng Xuân Lãm)
- **Operational control**: Marine Division, Airborne Division, 1st ARVN Division, 1st Ranger Group, 7th Armored Cavalry
- **Replacement of previous commander**: President Thieu replaced northern commander immediately after fall of Quảng Trị city
- **Strategic significance**: Ngô Quang Trưởng described as "one of ablest and most experienced ARVN commanders"
- Source: [HistoryNet – General Ngo Quang Truong; Wikipedia – Ngô Quang Trưởng](https://historynet.com/general-ngo-quang-truong-republic-vietnams-finest-commander/)
- **Confidence**: HIGH

### Lam Sơn 72 Operation Plan & Execution
- **Launch date**: 28 June 1972
- **Force composition**: ~30,000 ARVN troops + US advisors
- **Tactical approach**: 
  - 1st ARVN Division pushed **westward toward Laos** (parallel to DMZ)
  - Airborne Division & Marine Division pushed **north toward Quảng Trị city** (from south/east)
  - Heliborne assaults to insert troops behind NVA positions
  - Combined ground + air + artillery + naval gunfire
- **Timeline to Quảng Trị outskirts**:
  - By **7 July 1972** (~10 days): ARVN reached southern outskirts of Quảng Trị city
  - **11 July 1972**: CH-53 heliborne assault — 840 ARVN Marines inserted; 1 CH-53D shot down by SA-7 missile (3 US KIA, 45 ARVN Marines KIA)
  - **Late July**: Marines began entering citadel from east; heavy NVA counterassaults; ~27 July Airborne Division replaced by Marine Division (rotation/exhaustion)
  - **16 September 1972**: Citadel fully recaptured
- Source: [Wikipedia – Second Battle of Quảng Trị; HistoryNet – Vietnam's Longest Battle](https://historynet.com/vietnams-longest-battle/); [VHPA – CH-53 incident 11 July 1972](https://www.vhpa.org/KIA/incident/72071110KIA.HTM)
- **Confidence**: HIGH (US sourced); HIGH (dates matched)

## 3. The 81-Day Siege: Citadel Defense & Bombardment (28 June – 16 September 1972)

### Quảng Trị Citadel: Physical Specifications
- **Historical construction**: 
  - Built 1809 under King **Gia Long** (relocated from Tiên Kiên to Thạch Hãn commune)
  - Rebuilt in brick **1837 under King Minh Mạng**
  - Only two kings' reigns span: 1809–1837 construction
- **Architecture**:
  - **Square fortress** with outer and inner walls
  - **Wall height**: ~4 meters; **perimeter**: ~2,000 meters
  - **Wall thickness at base**: 13.5 meters; **parapet**: 0.7 meters
  - **Moat system**: Surrounding fortress with corners reinforced by 4 corner forts
  - **Gates**: 4 main gates (east, west, south, north), each 3.4 meters wide, arched
  - **Materials**: Large baked bricks bound with lime, molasses, additives
- **Strategic position**: Controls Route 1 corridor; crossroads of Thạch Hãn River (~100m east of citadel)
- Source: [Wikipedia – Citadel of Quảng Trị; HURACARS Quang Tri Citadel Guide](https://huracars.com/quang-tri-citadel/)
- **Confidence**: HIGH (architectural specs consistent)

### PAVN Defending Units & Commanders
- **Primary defending divisions** (theo phía Mỹ / Western sources):
  - **308th Infantry Division** (attacked 30 March; some elements at citadel defense)
  - **320th Division** (participant, July-Sept 1972)
  - **325th Division** (primary: 3 regiments — 18th, 95th, 101st — sent 12 July 1972 to reinforce)
  - **312th Division** (sent 12 July 1972 to support)
  - **324th Division** (324B — early Easter Offensive; may have held positions)
  - Other units: Independent Tri-Thien Regiment, local militia/sapper units
  - (theo phía Việt Nam / Vietnamese: Sư đoàn 320B, 325, 308, 304, 18, 27, 10 đặc công, v.v. — verify specific designations in Vietnamese official histories)
- **Commander**: Not clearly identified in Western sources; PAVN General Võ Nguyên Giáp oversaw Northern region strategy
- Source: [Wikipedia – 325th Division (Vietnam); 320th Division (Vietnam)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/325th_Division_(Vietnam))
- **Confidence**: HIGH for division names; MEDIUM for exact regiment assignments (Vietnamese sources may provide more detail)

### Bombardment & Tonnage
- **Total tonnage (88-day period June 28 – Sept 16, 1972)**: 
  - **Vietnamese claim**: **328,000 tons bombs + ammunition** (công bố chính thức Việt Nam)
  - **Comparison**: Equivalent to ~7 atomic bombs dropped on Hiroshima/Nagasaki (1945)
  - **Western/US claim**: More conservative; ~20,000-40,000+ tons total for region (specific citadel tonnage not precisely stated)
  - **B-52 Arc Light missions**: 70 B-52s dropped ~2,000 tons in 24-hour period; 60 B-52s hit targets around Firebase Anne (exact citadel tonnage disputed)
- **Intensity**: Most intensive bombing of a single province in Vietnam War
- **Source**: [Vietnam.vn – How many tons of bombs; Wikipedia – Operation Arc Light; DanViet – Khủng khiếp số lượng bom](https://www.vietnam.vn/en/thanh-co-quang-tri-phai-hung-chiu-bao-nhieu-tan-bom-dan-trong-81-ngay-dem)
- **Confidence**: HIGH for Vietnamese figure (official); MEDIUM–HIGH for Arc Light tonnage (US Air Force records); UNRESOLVED for exact US-side estimate of citadel-specific tonnage (Western sources give regional figures, not citadel-only)

### Defending Forces Rotation & Casualties
- **Initial garrison**: ARVN 3rd Division (collapsed May 1972)
- **Counter-offensive forces** (retaking):
  - **ARVN Marine Division** (entered late July, primary assault; withdrew Sept 14-16 across river)
  - **ARVN Airborne Division** (early assault; exhausted/rotated ~27 July)
  - **US advisors**: Embedded; air/artillery coordinators
- **NVA siege losses** (theo phía Mỹ):
  - **17,819 KIA/WIA** (theo phía Việt Nam: figure may differ; often refer to "hàng vạn" casualties)
- **ARVN losses**:
  - **7,756 KIA/WIA** (official count)
  - **20 US personnel KIA** (advisors)
  - Marine Division particularly depleted: ~5,000+ casualties est.
- **Significant single event**: CH-53 shot down 11 July — 3 US + 45 ARVN Marines killed
- Source: [Wikipedia – Second Battle of Quảng Trị; HistoryNet; Vietnam.vn](https://vietnamnews.vn/sunday/features/1176698/50-years-on-remembering-the-battle-of-quang-tri.html)
- **Confidence**: HIGH for overall totals; MEDIUM for NVA figures (sources vary; Vietnamese may claim higher; US may undercount)

### Duration Claim: "81 days and nights"
- **Official dates**: 28 June – 16 September 1972
- **Day count**: Exactly 81 days
- **Narrative significance**: Phrase "81 ngày đêm" (81 days and nights) entered Vietnamese historical lexicon; used in all official commemoration and the 2025 film "Mưa đỏ" (title alludes to this battle; shot in exactly 81 days on set)
- **Night crossings**: The Thạch Hãn River became critical supply line; reinforcements and supplies crossed at night; many soldiers drowned/killed in river (exact casualty figures unclear; not documented in available Western sources)
- Source: [Vietnam.vn; Wikipedia – Second Battle of Quảng Trị](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Qu%E1%BA%A3ng_Tr%E1%BB%8B)
- **Confidence**: HIGH for date range; HIGH for numerical "81 days"; MEDIUM for night-crossing casualty details (Vietnamese sources may have more)

### Withdrawal & Flag Raising
- **NVA withdrawal**: Across Thạch Hãn River northward (night of 14–15 Sept; exact timing varies by source)
- **ARVN entry to citadel**: Marines breached southeastern corner Sept 14
- **Flag ceremony**:
  - **Night of 15 September 1972**: ARVN Marines raised flag (preliminary)
  - **Official flag ceremony**: **16 September 1972, 12:45 PM** — South Vietnamese flag raised over **west gate** of citadel
  - **Duration of NVA occupation**: 138 days total (2 May capture – 16 Sept recapture)
- **Significance**: End of longest single battle of Vietnam War
- Source: [Wikipedia – Second Battle of Quảng Trị; History.com – Sept 16](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/september-15/)
- **Confidence**: HIGH for date; MEDIUM for exact time (some sources cite only "Sept 15-16"; 12:45 PM from one source)

## 4. US/Allied Support: Air, Naval, Artillery

### B-52 Arc Light Operations
- **Mission scope**: Intensive carpet bombing of NVA positions around Quảng Trị
- **Specific tonnage**:
  - 70 B-52s: ~2,000 tons in single 24-hour period
  - 48 B-52s: ~1,200 tons near Đồng Hới
  - 60 B-52s: Targets around Firebase Anne (support for ARVN Airborne)
- **Total B-52 tonnage (all Vietnam Theater)**: Arc Light operated until Aug 15, 1973 (126,615 sorties)
- **Citadel-specific tonnage**: Unclear; included in regional ~20,000–40,000 ton estimates
- **Platform variants**: B-52D, B-52F, B-52G
- Source: [Wikipedia – Operation Arc Light; Air and Space Forces Magazine](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/PDF/MagazineArchive/Documents/2009/January%202009/0109arc.pdf)
- **Confidence**: MEDIUM (regional figures clear; citadel-specific tonnage not precisely documented in Western sources)

### US Navy Gunfire Support: USS Newport News (CA-148)
- **Heavy cruiser**: Only heavy cruiser in US Navy at time; maximum long-range firepower
- **Deployment**: Returned to Vietnam April 1972 for third and final deployment
- **Station off Quảng Trị**: 13 miles north-northeast of Quảng Trị City
- **Service period**: "On the gun line" from 11 May 1972 onward; continued through campaign
- **Total tonnage delivered**: >50,000 major-caliber rounds over 525 missions against ~4,000 targets (entire deployment)
- **Tragedy**: 1 October 1972 — Turret explosion; faulty projectile detonated; 20 sailors KIA, 36 wounded
- **Post-mission**: After repairs, returned to gun line twice more before departure
- Source: [USNI – Fire in Turret Two; USS Newport News official; Navy History](https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/2012/october/fire-turret-two)
- **Confidence**: HIGH (naval records official; turret explosion well-documented)

### Tactical Air Support
- **F-4 Phantom**: Air Force and Navy; MiG combat patrol (MiGCAP), bombing runs
- **A-7 Corsair II**: Daylight strikes
- **A-6 Intruder**: Attack missions
- **Total tactical sorties**: ~650 daytime strikes (complementing ~700 B-52 night sorties)
- **Estimated tactical losses**: 5–12 aircraft (US count; VN claim higher)
- Source: [HistoryNet; Air Force records](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/article/1212linebacker/)
- **Confidence**: MEDIUM (regional data; Quảng Trị-specific sorties not separately detailed)

### Thạch Hãn River Crossings & Marine Operations
- **River width**: Not precisely specified in sources; serves as DMZ/supply line
- **By Oct 1972**: ARVN held line along Thạch Hãn (south bank); attempted northward push limited
- **VNMC Brigade 369**: 6th VNMC Battalion crossed Thạch Hãn directly with sampans; 600 Marines
- **Limited gains**: By end 1972, ARVN/Marines held positions ~5 km south of river; further advance across halted
- **Casualty implications**: "Many drowned/killed in river" (Vietnamese sources); exact numbers unavailable in Western records
- Source: [Wikipedia – Second Battle of Quảng Trị; USMCU – Vietnam Marines and Defense of Quang Tri 1972](https://www.usmcu.edu/Outreach/Marine-Corps-University-Press/MCH/Marine-Corps-History-Summer-2015/Vietnam-Marines-and-the-Defense-of-Quang-Tri-in-1972/)
- **Confidence**: HIGH for operational facts; MEDIUM for specific casualty figures (Vietnamese sources needed)

## 5. Human Details: Poems, Letters, Film, Commemorations

### Lê Bá Dương & Poem "Lời người bên sông" (Words from the Riverbank)
- **Author**: Lê Bá Dương (Cựu chiến binh / veteran)
- **Military role during battle**: Company commander at Quảng Trị Citadel, age 19 in 1972
- **Poem composition date**: **27 July 1987** — written 15 years after battle (NOT during battle)
- **Occasion**: Written on afternoon of 27 July, after flower-floating ceremony (lễ thả hoa đăng) honoring fallen soldiers on Thạch Hãn River
- **First publication**: July 1990 in journal "Science and Technology of Khánh Hòa" (occasion: Ngày Thương binh Liệt sĩ / Wounded Soldiers & Fallen Heroes Day)
- **Poem text (verified)**:
  ```
  Đò xuôi Thạch Hãn, ơi… chèo nhẹ
  Đáy sông còn đó, bạn tôi nằm
  Có tuổi hai mươi thành sóng nước
  Vỗ yên bờ, mãi mãi ngàn năm
  ```
  (Literal: "Ferrying down the Thạch Hãn, oh... paddle gently / My friends still lie at the bottom / At twenty years old, they became waves / Resting gently on shore, forever a thousand years")
- **Legacy**: Engraved on stone tablets at flower-floating platform both banks of Thạch Hãn River
- **Adaptations**: >20 musical versions (choral, song, rock, traditional vocal)
- **Symbolic meaning**: Poem became symbol of lost youth & sacrifice during the 81-day battle
- Source: [Xây dựng Đảng – Cựu chiến binh Lê Bá Dương; Tiền Phong – Lời người bên sông còn mãi](https://xaydungdang.org.vn/van-hoa-xa-hoi/cuu-chien-binh-le-ba-duong-va-bai-tho-loi-nguoi-ben-song-15347)
- **Confidence**: HIGH (poem and dates verified; author biography confirmed)

### Today's Site: Khu di tích (Heritage Site) Status
- **Current designation**: **Di tích quốc gia đặc biệt** (Special National Heritage Site)
- **Designation date**: **9 December 2013** (Prime Minister's Decision No. 2383/QĐ-TTg)
- **Scope**: Citadel + memorial sites of 1972 81-day event
- **Restoration history**:
  - **1993–1995**: Province restored citadel; concrete/basalt retaining walls constructed
  - **1993 onward**: Bridge restored per original architecture; moat system rehabilitated
  - **2011**: New cement-reinforced concrete low walls; moat dredged; water channeled in; flowers floating aesthetic (lễ thả hoa đăng annual)
- **Physical condition post-1972**: Heavily damaged; only eastern gate and sections of walls relatively intact
- **Memorial infrastructure**:
  - Tượng Đài (Monument) erected in citadel
  - Bến thả hoa Thạch Hãn (Flower-floating platform) on river banks
  - Annual **27 July ceremonies** (Ngày Thương binh Liệt sĩ / Wounded Soldiers & Fallen Heroes Day); "Đêm hoa đăng Quảng Trị" (Quảng Trị Flower-Floating Night)
- **2025 Administrative change**: **Quảng Bình merged into Quảng Trị on 12 June 2025** (nationwide provincial restructuring); admin center now Đông Hới (formerly center of Quảng Bình); Quảng Trị Citadel remains major heritage attraction in merged province
- Source: [Bảo Tàng Chứng Tích Chiến Tranh; Quảng Trị official; Wikipedia – Special National Heritage Sites (Vietnam)](https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Di_t%C3%ADch_qu%E1%BB%91c_gia_%C4%91%E1%BA%B1c_bi%E1%BB%87t_(Vi%E1%BB%87t_Nam))
- **Confidence**: HIGH (official designations); HIGH (2025 merger confirmed)

## 6. Paris Peace Accords & Aftermath

### Paris Peace Accords (27 January 1973)
- **Official title**: Agreement on Ending the War and Restoring Peace in Viet-Nam
- **Signatories**: USA, North Vietnam (DRV), South Vietnam (RVN), Viet Cong (PRG)
- **Key terms**:
  - **Ceasefire**: In-place ceasefire throughout South Vietnam as of 24:00 GMT 27 January 1973
  - **US withdrawal**: All US combat forces withdrawn within **60 days** (completed March 1973)
  - **POW release**: N. Vietnam to release US prisoners (Operation Homecoming: 591 POWs)
  - **DMZ**: 17th Parallel maintained as **provisional dividing line**
  - **Reunification**: To occur through "peaceful means"; "eventual reunification" acknowledged
  - **Territory**: NVA permitted to remain in-place in South Vietnam (unprecedented in US negotiation history)
- **Border arrangement post-Accords**: ARVN held southern Thạch Hãn line; PRG/NVA held north of river; remained stable until 1975
- **Outcome**: Agreement closely resembled October 1972 draft; fighting did NOT cease after 28 Jan 1973; ceasefire collapsed by 1974
- **Significance for Quảng Trị**: Citadel remained under ARVN control until 1975 Fall of Saigon; part of final frontier negotiations
- Source: [Britannica – Paris Peace Accords; Wikipedia; US State Dept; History.com](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Paris-Peace-Accords)
- **Confidence**: HIGH (treaty text official)

### War of the Flags (1972–1975 Post-Accords)
- **Thạch Hãn River**: Became de facto border; ARVN (south bank) vs PAVN (north bank)
- **Flag competition**: Symbolic war continued after 1973 Accords; flags raised/removed/re-raised at various points (ongoing ritual)
- **1975**: Final NVA offensive; Quảng Trị fell to North; flag finally raised north of Thạch Hãn permanently
- Source: [Wikipedia – War of the Flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_flags)
- **Confidence**: MEDIUM–HIGH (historical narrative; exact dates/details vary)
