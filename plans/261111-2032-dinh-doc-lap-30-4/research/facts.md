## 1. Context: Paris Accords to Spring Offensives (Jan–April 1975)

### Paris Peace Accords (27 January 1973)
- **Official title**: Agreement on Ending the War and Restoring Peace in Viet-Nam
- **Signatories**: USA, North Vietnam (DRV), South Vietnam (RVN), Viet Cong (PRG)
- **Key terms**:
  - Ceasefire in-place throughout South Vietnam (entered force 24:00 GMT 27 Jan 1973)
  - US withdrawal of all combat forces within 60 days (completed March 1973); ~23,700 troops total
  - NVA permitted to remain in-place in South Vietnam (unprecedented)
  - DMZ maintained as provisional dividing line; eventual reunification "through peaceful means" acknowledged
  - POW release: 591 Americans (Operation Homecoming)
- **Outcome**: Ceasefire violated by 1974; fighting continued despite agreement
- Source: [Britannica – Paris Peace Accords](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Paris-Peace-Accords); [Wikipedia – Paris Peace Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Accords)
- **Confidence**: HIGH

### 1975 Spring Offensives: Campaign Sequence
- **Phước Long (6 January 1975)**: NVA captured first provincial capital; ARVN forces collapsed; fewer than 1/6 survived
  - Source: [Wikipedia – 1975 Spring Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_spring_offensive)
  - **Confidence**: HIGH
- **Tây Nguyên Campaign (March 1975)**: Codename "Campaign 275"; attacked from Cambodia
  - **Buôn Ma Thuột**: NVA attacked 10 March; captured city 18 March (8 days of fighting)
  - Source: [Wikipedia – Battle of Ban Me Thuot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ban_Me_Thuot)
  - **Confidence**: HIGH
- **Huế–Đà Nẵng Campaign**:
  - Huế liberated 26 March 1975
  - Đà Nẵng fell 29–30 March 1975
  - 70,000 ARVN troops captured during campaign
  - Source: [Wikipedia – 1975 Spring Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_spring_offensive)
  - **Confidence**: HIGH
- **Xuân Lộc Battle (9–21 April 1975)**: Last major conventional battle
  - ARVN 18th Division (Sư đoàn 18) under Brigadier General Lê Minh Đảo defended strategic crossroads
  - PAVN 4th Army Corps (Quân đoàn 4) under Major General Hoàng Cầm attacked
  - ARVN losses: ~30% casualties (~4,000 soldiers); NVA casualties: ~5,000+
  - Order to withdraw 19 April; forces heavily damaged
  - Source: [Wikipedia – Battle of Xuân Lộc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Xu%C3%A2n_L%E1%BB%99c); [AsTheySawIt – Battle of Xuân Lộc](https://astheysawit.com/events/battle-of-xuan-loc-last-major-battle-vietnam-war/)
  - **Confidence**: HIGH

### South Vietnamese Political Transition
- **21 April 1975**: President Nguyễn Văn Thiệu resigns in bitter speech; blames USA ("You have asked us to do something you failed to do, with half a million troops and $300 billion expenditure"). Trần Văn Hương becomes interim president
  - Source: [Britannica – Nguyễn Văn Thiệu](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nguyen-Van-Thieu)
  - **Confidence**: HIGH
- **28 April 1975 afternoon** (some sources say ~16:00–17:00): Dương Văn Minh becomes president; Trần Văn Hương steps aside. Minh's presidency lasts only 48 hours
  - Source: [Wikipedia – Dương Văn Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C6%B0%C6%A1ng_V%C4%83n_Minh)
  - **Confidence**: MEDIUM (sources vary on exact time; ~afternoon established)

## 2. Chiến dịch Hồ Chí Minh (26–30 April 1975)

### Campaign Naming & Command
- **14 April 1975**: North Vietnamese Politburo renamed campaign "Chiến dịch Hồ Chí Minh" (Ho Chi Minh Campaign) hoping to complete victory before Hồ Chí Minh's birthday (19 May). Prior codename: "Campaign 275" or "Tây Nguyên Campaign"
  - Source: [Vietnam News – Spring 1975 General Offensive](https://vietnamnews.vn/politics-laws/1780359/spring-1975-general-offensive-and-uprising-a-decisive-strategic-turning-point.html); [Wikipedia – 1975 Spring Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_spring_offensive)
  - **Confidence**: HIGH
- **Senior command** (theo phía Việt Nam):
  - **Commander (Tư lệnh)**: General Văn Tiến Dũng
  - **Political Commissar (Chính ủy)**: Phạm Hùng
  - **Senior advisor**: Lê Đức Thọ
  - **Deputy/Regional commanders**: Trần Văn Trà (Deputy Commander A75 headquarters), Lê Trọng Tấn, Nguyễn Hữu An, Hoàng Cầm
  - Source: [Wikipedia – Fall of Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Saigon); [Wikipedia – Trần Văn Trà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%E1%BA%A7n_V%C4%83n_Tr%C3%A0)
  - **Confidence**: HIGH

### PAVN Forces & Operational Directions
- **5 attack directions** (5 hướng/cánh quân) converging on Saigon–Gia Định:
  1. **Quân đoàn 1** (1st Corps): from north → attacked Bộ Tổng tham mưu (Ministry of Defense HQ)
  2. **Quân đoàn 2** (2nd Corps, Hương Giang): from east/southeast → attacked **Dinh Độc Lập** (primary objective). Includes Tank Brigade 203, 304th Infantry Division, 306th Infantry Division, 325th Infantry Division
  3. **Quân đoàn 3** (3rd Corps): from northwest → attacked Tân Sơn Nhất Air Base
  4. **Quân đoàn 4** (4th Corps, Nguyễn Hữu An): northeast/east → attacked Bộ Quốc phòng (Ministry of Defense)
  5. **Special forces/local troops**: southwest → attacked Biệt khu Thủ đô (Capital Special Zone), Tổng nha cảnh sát (National Police HQ)
- **Campaign start time**: 17:00 (5 PM) on 26 April 1975
- Source: [Wikipedia – 1975 Spring Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_spring_offensive); [Vietnam.vn – The Rapid Journey](https://www.vietnam.vn/en/chang-duong-than-toc)
- **Confidence**: HIGH

### Defender Forces
- **ARVN commanders** (by this point, chaos reigning):
  - Dương Văn Minh took office 28 April; ordered surrender 30 April to prevent urban combat
  - Capital region defenders: Capital Military District (Quân khu Thủ đô) under nominal control; units fragmented or surrendering
  - Air Base (Tân Sơn Nhất): 2 US Marine guards (McMahon, Judge) killed 29 April by rocket fire; no organized defense
- Source: [Wikipedia – Fall of Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Saigon)
- **Confidence**: MEDIUM (ARVN command structure collapsed; exact unit-level data incomplete)

## 3. US Evacuation: Operation Frequent Wind (29–30 April 1975)

### Evacuation Overview
- **Duration**: ~19 hours (late 29 April – early 30 April)
- **Total evacuated**: 1,373 Americans + 5,595 Vietnamese and third-country nationals = **~7,000 total**
- **Helicopter sorties**: 630+ total (444 USAF/USMC, 122 from Defense Attaché compound, 72 from Embassy)
- **Aircraft carrier landing**: USS Midway received final helicopters; aircraft pushed overboard to make deck space
- Source: [USNI – Operation Frequent Wind](https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history/2025/april/bravery-chaos-operation-frequent-wind-april-1975); [Wikipedia – Operation Frequent Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Frequent_Wind)
- **Confidence**: HIGH

### Key Moments
- **03:30 on 29 April**: Rocket attack at Tân Sơn Nhất DAO compound kills last two US combat casualties: **Marine Corporal Charles McMahon** and **Lance Corporal Darwin Judge** (Embassy guard duty; in-country only 1 week)
  - Source: [SOFREP – McMahon & Judge](https://sofrep.com/specialoperations/remembering-marines-charles-mcmahon-darwin-judge-last-american-kias-in-vietnam-april-29-1975/); [Wikipedia – Operation Frequent Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Frequent_Wind)
  - **Confidence**: HIGH
- **29 April late morning**: Decision made to evacuate all US personnel by helicopter
- **~16:05 on 28 April**: A-37 Quyết Thắng Squadron raid on Tân Sơn Nhất paralyzed airfield (24 RVNAF aircraft destroyed, 300+ casualties); precluded fixed-wing evacuation
  - Source: [nhandan.vn – Phi đội Quyết Thắng](https://special.nhandan.vn/Phi-doi-Quyet-thang-danh-vao-san-bay-Tan-son-Nhat/)
  - **Confidence**: HIGH
- **~04:58 AM on 30 April**: **Ambassador Graham Martin** boards last CH-46 helicopter "Lady Ace 09" after 15 attempts to extract him (President Ford personally ordered departure)
  - Radio signal: "Tiger is out" (code phrase; interpreted by other pilots as mission complete, causing delay in final Marine extraction)
  - Source: [Smithsonian – Last Men Out](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/last-men-out-77127449/); [RetroNewser](https://retronewser.com/2025/04/29/u-s-ambassador-graham-martin-flees-vietnam/)
  - **Confidence**: HIGH
- **07:53 AM on 30 April**: **Final helicopter "Swift 2-2" or "Swift 22"** lifts off US Embassy rooftop with 11 last Marines of security detachment (call sign variants between sources)
  - Source: [USNI – Frequent Wind](https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history/2025/april/bravery-chaos-operation-frequent-wind-april-1975)
  - **Confidence**: HIGH (date/time consistent; exact call sign MEDIUM)

### Iconic Photograph: Hubert van Es Rooftop Photo
- **Location**: 22 Gia Long Street (NOT US Embassy), Pittman Apartments, downtown Saigon
- **Aircraft**: Bell Model 204B helicopter (Air America)
- **Photographer**: Dutch photojournalist Hubert van Es (1941–2009)
- **Photo date**: 29 April 1975 (some sources cite ambiguity)
- **Mislabeling**: Assistant mislabeled photo as "US Embassy" in rush to distribute; Van Es later clarified correct location
- **Significance**: Most iconic image of Fall of Saigon
- Source: [Wikipedia – 22 Gia Long Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22_Gia_Long_Street); [Medium – Vinh Ho](https://medium.com/@vinhtheho/i-visited-the-famous-vietnam-war-photo-site-helicopter-on-the-rooftop-this-is-what-i-learned-dd90ae62ac97)
- **Confidence**: HIGH

### Major Lý Bửng's Cessna Landing on USS Midway
- **30 April 1975**: VNAF Major Lý Bửng flew Cessna O-1 "Bird Dog" (L-19) reconnaissance aircraft from Con Son Island with wife and children aboard (6 people total)
- **Event**: Spotted USS Midway (CV-41) aircraft carrier; low on fuel (~1 hour remaining); dropped handwritten note requesting landing clearance
- **Action**: USS Midway Captain Lawrence Chambers ordered UH-1 Huey helicopters pushed overboard to clear flight deck space (exact number varies; sources cite 15–20 helicopters pushed)
- **Landing**: Lý Bửng successfully landed O-1 on carrier deck at ~13:00–15:00; became the first VNAF fixed-wing aircraft to land aboard a US carrier; symbolic final VNAF flight
- **Crew status**: Lý Bửng was initially identified as "Captain" in some sources; military records confirm "Major" rank
- **Aircraft location**: Original O-1 on display at National Museum of Naval Aviation, Pensacola, Florida
- Source: [VNAF Historical Society Blog – Major Lý Bửng](http://rvnhs.blogspot.com/2015/08/vnaf-major-ly-bungs-flight-to-freedom.html); [Wikipedia – 1975 in Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_in_the_Vietnam_War); [history.navy.mil – O-1 Bird Dog](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/museums/nnam/explore/collections/aircraft/o/o-1-bird-dog.html)
- **Confidence**: HIGH

## 4. Morning April 30, 1975: Hour by Hour (Tanks & Gate Breaking)

### Pre-Dawn & Early Morning
- **Before 10:45 AM**: Quân đoàn 2 (2nd Corps) Tank Brigade 203 (Lữ đoàn xe tăng 203) advances from east along **Xa lộ Biên Hòa** (Biên Hòa Highway) toward city center
  - Brigade commander: **Nguyễn Tất Tài**
  - Political commissar (Chính ủy): **Bùi Văn Tùng** (rank: Lieutenant Colonel)
  - **Lữ đoàn 203** composition: T-54B tanks (Soviet), Type 59 tanks (Chinese variant); T-55 tanks
- Source: [dinhdoclap.gov.vn – History](https://dinhdoclap.gov.vn/en/history-of-the-independent-palace/); [TAPCQPTD – Tank Forces](https://m.tapchiqptd.vn/en/theory-and-practice/tank-armoured-force-during-1975-spring-general-offensive-and-uprising-24047.html)
- **Confidence**: HIGH

### **~10:45–10:50 AM – Tank 843 Hits Side Gate**
- **Tank number**: 843 (Soviet T-54B)
- **Crew**:
  - **Commander**: Senior Lieutenant (Trung úy) **Bùi Quang Thận** (born 10 Oct 1948, Thái Bình province; died 24 June 2012)
  - **Gunner 1**: **Thái Bá Minh**
  - **Gunner 2**: **Nguyễn Văn Kỷ** (from Nghệ An province)
  - **Driver**: **Lữ Văn Hỏa** (from Mễ Nội, Phủ Lý, Hà Nam province)
- **Position**: Moving along **Lê Duẩn Street** (also called Thống Nhất Street) toward palace
- **Action**: Rammed through **side gate (cổng phụ bên trái)** of Independence Palace; became stuck
- **Note**: Tank 843 hit the side gate; Tank 390 hit main gate. Official Vietnamese accounts initially claimed 843 was "first to breach palace," but photographic evidence (Françoise Demulder, 1995) showed 390 was first through main gate.
- Source: [dinhdoclap.gov.vn – History](https://dinhdoclap.gov.vn/en/history-of-the-independent-palace/); [danviet.vn – Tank 843](https://danviet.vn/khoanh-khac-chiec-xe-tang-843-huc-vao-cong-phu-trai-dinh-doc-lap-trong-ngay-30-4-1975-20210429005555147-d946795.html); [svhttdl.hanam.gov.vn – Tank 843 Driver](https://svhttdl.hanam.gov.vn/Pages/nguoi-chien-sy-lai-xe-tang-843-huc-do-cong-phu-dinh-doc-lap-ngay-3041975.aspx)
- **Confidence**: HIGH

### **~11:00 AM – Tank 390 Breaches Main Gate**
- **Tank number**: 390 (Chinese Type 59, T-59 variant; misidentified as T-54 in some sources)
- **Crew** (verified names):
  - **Commander / Political officer** (Chính trị viên Đại đội): Captain **Vũ Đăng Toàn** (Trung úy)
  - **Driver**: **Nguyễn Văn Tập** (Trung sĩ / Sergeant, born ~1951, from Gia Lộc, Hải Dương province; alive as of April 2025)
  - **Gunner 1**: Sergeant **Ngô Sỹ Nguyên**
  - **Gunner 2 / Deputy commander**: Lieutenant **Lê Văn Phượng** (died 2016)
  - **Total crew**: 4 enlisted men + officers (3 surviving in 2025)
- **Action**: Tank 390 adjusted course, **smashed through the main gate** (cổng chính) of Independence Palace; first to enter palace grounds
- **Route**: Advanced down **Lê Duẩn Street / Thống Nhất Boulevard** (sources vary on street name used in 1975)
- **Timing note**: Sources cite "around 11:00 AM" or "shortly after 10:45 AM" for tank 390 breaking main gate
- Source: [thestar.com.my – Tank Crew Story](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2025/04/28/how-vietnam-tank-crew-that-crashed-into-saigon-palace-shaped-course-of-history-50-years-ago/); [doanhnghiepvn.vn – Type-59 Crew](https://doanhnghiepvn.vn/kham-pha/chan-dung-trung-uy-lai-xe-tang-type-59-mang-so-hieu-390-huc-do-cong-dinh-doc-lap-ngay-30-4-1975/20250425082428662.html)
- **Confidence**: HIGH (crew names verified); MEDIUM (exact time 10:45 vs 11:00; sources show "shortly after" or "around")

### **11:30 AM – Flag Raising**
- **Time**: 11:30 AM (Bùi Quang Thận wrote "11g30 ngày 30/4" on the flag he removed and kept)
- **Person**: Senior Lieutenant **Bùi Quang Thận** (commander of tank 843) climbed to palace roof
- **Flag removed**: South Vietnamese flag (Cờ Việt Nam Cộng hòa; 3 red stripes)—Thận carefully lowered it, folded it, signed the old flag with the date/time notation, and preserved it
- **Flag raised**: **National Liberation Front flag** (Cờ Mặt trận Dân tộc Giải phóng Miền Nam): **top half red, bottom half blue, yellow star in center** (designated by NLF 1st Congress, 16 February 1962 at Tân Biên, Tây Ninh)
- **Birthplace**: Thái Bình province
- **Later life**: Thận retired as Colonel; died 24 June 2012 (age 63–64); witnessed by many palace staff and documented in multiple Vietnamese sources
- **Note on flag preservation**: The old South Vietnamese flag that Thận removed and signed was kept and served as verification proof when matched against the tear on the remaining rope.
- Source: [thaibinh.gov.vn – Soldier from Thai Binh](https://thaibinh.gov.vn/en/news/people-and-place/soldier-from-thai-binh-who-put-flag-on-the-roof-of-independe.html); [vnexpress.net – Afternoon of April 30](https://vnexpress.net/buoi-trua-30-4-1975-tai-dinh-doc-lap-2032767.html); [thuvienphapluat.vn – NLF Flag](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/phap-luat/la-co-tung-bay-tren-noc-dinh-doc-lap-mau-gi-la-co-giai-phong-mien-nam-co-mau-gi-y-nghia-the-nao-311941-194037.html)
- **Confidence**: HIGH

### Surrender & Broadcasting
- **10:24 AM**: President Dương Văn Minh announced **ceasefire order** on radio, calling for cessation of hostilities and awaiting power transfer negotiations (first broadcast)
- **~13:20–14:30 (1:20–2:30 PM)**: Dương Văn Minh **read formal unconditional surrender declaration** at Saigon Radio Station; broadcast time varied between sources (13:20 per Saigon time, or 14:30 per alternative records). Officers at palace and radio station drafted the formal surrender statement.
- **At same time (13:20–14:30)**: **Bùi Văn Tùng** (Đại tá, chính ủy Lữ đoàn xe tăng 203; born 4 Feb 1930, died 9 Feb 2024) **read acceptance statement** on behalf of liberation forces at Saigon Radio, declaring the city "hoàn toàn giải phóng" (completely liberated). Bùi Văn Tùng participated in drafting the surrender declaration.
- **11:30 AM**: Meanwhile, **Bùi Quang Thận raised the NLF flag on the palace roof** (earlier event, but became the symbolic moment—"11:30 AM on 30 April 1975" is the moment most closely tied to the palace's fall in official commemorations)
- Source: [vnexpress.net – Broadcast on 30/4](https://vnexpress.net/buoi-phat-thanh-lich-su-tai-sai-gon-trua-30-4-1975-2193917.html); [baohaiphong.vn – Listen to 30/4/1975 broadcast](https://baohaiphong.vn/nghe-ban-tin-dac-biet-ngay-30-4-1975-410573.html); [vietnamplus.vn – Surrender statement](https://www.vietnamplus.vn/khoang-khac-va-su-kien-ngay-841975-phi-cong-nguyen-thanh-trung-nem-bom-dinh-doc-lap-post1025365.vnp); [znews.vn – Bùi Văn Tùng](https://znews.vn/nguyen-chinh-uy-lu-doan-xe-tang-203-bui-van-tung-qua-doi-post1400327.html)
- **Confidence**: MEDIUM–HIGH (10:24 AM ceasefire confirmed; broadcast time 13:20–14:30 has minor discrepancies between sources; 11:30 symbolic moment HIGH)

### Simultaneous Operations
- **Bộ Tổng tham mưu** (General Staff HQ): Attacked by Quân đoàn 1 (1st Corps)
- **Tân Sơn Nhất Air Base**: Under assault by Quân đoàn 3 (3rd Corps); previously damaged by 28 April Quyết Thắng A-37 raid
- **Bộ Quốc phòng** (Ministry of Defense): Targeted by Quân đoàn 4 (4th Corps)
- **Tổng nha cảnh sát** (National Police HQ): Southwest assault
- **Status**: By noon, multiple government centers breached; organized resistance ceased
- Source: [Wikipedia – Fall of Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Saigon)
- **Confidence**: MEDIUM (specific timings/units partial)

## 5. Aftermath & Reunification (May–July 1975)

### Mekong Delta Liberation (1–2 May 1975)
- **May 1**: Most Mekong Delta provinces liberated:
  - My Tho: 5:00 AM
  - Chau Doc: 8:30 AM
  - Vi Thanh: 9:30 AM
  - Ben Tre: noon
  - Ca Mau: morning
  - Long Xuyen: evening
- **May 2**: Con Dao Island liberated (night)
- **Campaign end**: Officially concluded 2 May 1975 (some sources: 30 April for Saigon fall, 1–2 May for Delta finish)
- Source: [nhandan.vn – Liberation of Mekong Delta](https://en.nhandan.vn/may-1-1975-liberation-of-the-remaining-provinces-in-the-mekong-delta-post147409.html)
- **Confidence**: HIGH

### Administrative Council & City Renaming
- **Immediately after 30 April**: Military Administrative Council (Ủy ban Quân quản) established for Saigon–Gia Định
  - Led by Trần Văn Trà (deputy commander Ho Chi Minh Campaign)
- **June 1975**: Political consultative conference held at Dinh Độc Lập (Independence Palace) for North–South reunification discussions
- **2 July 1976**: Vietnamese National Assembly (6th session, June 24 – July 3) officially **renamed city Saigon–Gia Định → Thành phố Hồ Chí Minh** (Ho Chi Minh City) + renamed country **CHDCNXHCN Việt Nam** (Socialist Republic of Vietnam, replacing "Democratic Republic of Vietnam" in north and "Republic of Vietnam" in south)
- **Dinh Độc Lập renamed**: **Hội trường Thống Nhất** (Reunification Hall) on **25 June 1976** (or official renaming with political significance tied to July 2 reunification vote)
- Source: [Wikipedia – Fall of Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Saigon); [vietnamnet.vn – Ho Chi Minh City's Name](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/the-untold-story-behind-ho-chi-minh-city-s-historic-name-2531328.html)
- **Confidence**: HIGH (dates of renaming consistent)

## 6. Present Day: Dinh Độc Lập / Hội trường Thống Nhất (2025–2026)

### Palace Designation & Status
- **Current official name**: **Hội trường Thống Nhất** (Reunification Hall); also called **Dinh Độc Lập** (Independence Palace) in historical context
- **Designation**: **Di tích quốc gia đặc biệt** (Special National Historical Site)
  - Originally: National Historical Site (1976)
  - Upgraded: Prime Minister Decision No. 1272/QĐ-TTg (12 August 2009) — designated as one of Vietnam's first 10 special national heritage sites
  - Source: [dinhdoclap.gov.vn](https://dinhdoclap.gov.vn/en/the-independence-palace-national-historic-site/); [Wikipedia – Independence Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Palace)
  - **Confidence**: HIGH

### Architecture & Restoration
- **Architect**: **Ngô Viết Thụ** (1914–2002; winner Grand Prix de Rome 1955, Beaux-Arts school, Paris)
- **Original palace (Dinh Norodom)**: Bombed **27 February 1962** by dissident RVNAF pilots **Nguyễn Văn Cử** and **Phạm Phú Quốc**; left wing destroyed; aircraft: AD-6 bombers
- **New palace construction**: 
  - Began: 1 July 1962 (ordered by Pres. Ngô Đình Diệm)
  - Completed: 31 October 1966
  - Design: Modern European + traditional East Asian harmony (Beaux-Arts modernist + symbolic Eastern philosophy)
- **Post-1975 restoration**:
  - Preserved as-is with minor repairs; furniture, maps, war maps left in place
  - Gate rebuilt after tank breach
  - Replicas of tanks 390 and 843 installed at palace entrance
